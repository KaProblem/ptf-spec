# Changelog

All notable changes to the PTF specification and tools. Versions follow semver;
before 1.0, minor versions may break compatibility.

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- Media (SPEC.md §6.9): audio and video are content that the author makes once; hosts only play them. An element lists them in `media` (`id`, `kind`, `src` as `media/<file>` up to 10 MiB or an `https` URL, `sha256`, `bytes`, `duration_s`, `transcript` as `.txt` or `.vtt` (required for video), `license`, optional `title`, `language`, `speakers`, `generator`, `objectives`, `levels`, `use_when`). Question blocks take `media` and `plays`. PEDAGOGY.md lists audio and video as a medium.
- `ptf-check` checks media: hash, size, the 10 MiB limit, file type, transcript present and plausible for the length, kind matches the file, duration (when `ffprobe` is installed), questions name existing media, unlisted files in `media/`. New `tools/ptf-media.mjs entry <bundle> <file>` prints the fields of an entry.
- MCP server: `show_media` plays an element's audio or video in the quiz card; `show_question` takes `question_ids` for several questions in one card (e.g. all items of a listening task) and plays their shared media with its play limit. The card sends one message once all questions are answered and shows the transcript only then; the tutor gets the transcript with the instruction not to reveal it. Local files reach the card as a `data:` URL through the app-only `ptf_media_source` (the default MCP Apps CSP allows `media-src data:`), after a `sha256` check; `https` media are passed on as URLs. Playing a medium to the end records `experienced` on `ptf:el/…#media:<id>`.
- Example library: listening text `sam-apples` (12 s, generated with Piper) with two questions in the demo bundle (0.4.0); browser test `mcp/test/media.browser.mjs`.
- Answer checks (SPEC.md §7.1): `checks/<element>.js` recomputes the answers of an element's questions; `ptf-check` runs it in a sandboxed `node:vm` context, reports every difference as an error and warns about computed answers (`estimate_number`, `calculation_steps`, numeric `cloze` and `guess_next`) without a check. `fact(text, fn)` checks numbers and claims in the element's text. The author skill requires them. The minimal example has one.
- Figures (SPEC.md §6.8): a bundle's `figures/<id>.svg`, listed by elements in `figures` (`id`, `objectives`, `levels`, `use_when`). One self-describing SVG with `<title>` and `<desc>` (what the tutor knows about it), optional steps `<g data-step data-caption>` that the host reveals one by one, colours and fonts only through classes mapped to theme tokens (`runtime/ptf-figure.css`), viewBox at most 360 units wide, no scripts, styles, images, links or animations; translations as `<id>.<lang>.svg` with the same steps. PEDAGOGY.md §5 lists figures as a medium.
- `ptf-check` checks figures (format rules, steps 1..n, `#id` references, translations keep the steps, `figures[]` entries resolve and name real objectives, unreferenced figures). New `tools/ptf-figure.mjs`: `check` for single files, `shot` renders a figure at 320 px (every step, light and dark) and 640 px and reports overlapping or clipped text and the smallest text size on a phone.
- MCP server: `show_figure` (`element`, `figure`, optional `lang`, `step`) shows a figure as an MCP App card (`ui://ptf/figure.html`) with caption, Back / Next / Show all and the description. The tutor gets the description and every caption, the card tells it the current step and records `experienced` with object `ptf:el/…#figure:<id>` once every step was seen. The card keeps only the elements and attributes of §6.8. The learner's `preferred_lang` picks a translation. App-only `ptf_figure_source` for hosts that drop `_meta`.
- Skill `ptf-figure`: plan in words first, compute coordinates in a script kept as `figures/src/<id>.*` that also asserts the numbers it labels, then a mandatory screenshot check at phone width, light and dark. The tutor and author skills point to figures.
- Example library: figure `count-apples` (+ German translation) in the demo bundle.
- Quiz card (`mcp/src/quiz.html`): cards for `cloze`, `ordering`, `estimate_number`, `guess_next` and `calculation_steps`. The card checks and records them like `multiple_choice`; numbers are read in the learner's locale (`1,5`, `1.5e9`, `2.83 · 10⁻³`). `estimate_number` shows the guess against the answer and its tolerance band; `calculation_steps` lets the learner work out each step's value in turn. `show_question` accepts every kind.
- `vendor/`: allowlisted build `chess.js@1.4.0` (BSD-2-Clause, global `ChessJS`, 37 KB) for board simulators that check legal moves, check and mate. Rules only; an engine such as Stockfish stays out of simulators (WebAssembly is blocked by the CSP).

### Changed
- Tutor skill and `show_question`: every check, probe, warmup and review question is asked as a card, not in the chat; chat questions are for open "why" talk.
- SPEC.md §7: `estimate_number.tolerance` is absolute, in the unit of `answer`; the last column of a `calculation_steps` row is the value the learner works out.
- LEARNER-RECORD.md 0.3: the record is its events; profile, completions, consents, courses and the review estimate are derived from them, so a sync that merges events by `id` is lossless and two hosts derive the same state. Profile changes are `profile_updated` events (merged per field in event order; a 0.2 `profile.md` is imported once); `keep` and `note` are event fields. The export is `events.jsonl`; `profile.md` and `mastery.json` are views.
- Completion (LEARNER-RECORD.md §5): a `completed` event is per element **and level**, names the objectives shown and the correct `answered` events that show them (`result.objectives`, `result.evidence`), and carries `context.version` and `context.catalog`. Hosts reject invalid completions when recorded and ignore them when derived; 0.2 completions without evidence count as `verified: false`. Going deeper sets a target level and never changes a completion; review answers never reopen a done step.
- No decision depends on the mastery number any more (LEARNER-RECORD.md §6): it is the host's estimate for reviews only. The shared 0.8 "secure" threshold and `source: "diagnosis"` are gone; a step shown before teaching is `completed` with `context.reason: placement`, and `assessed` only plans levels and reviews.
- Skipping (SPEC.md §8.1): `skipped` (the learner leaves a step out; does not count) is distinct from a completion shown before teaching (counts).
- Sharing (LEARNER-RECORD.md §7, SPEC.md §8.2): `consented` and `withdrew` events per course and concrete recipient (catalog ID or URL), with areas; hosts derive the current consents and must check them before sharing. `sharing.with: author` is removed (ambiguous with several authors); `publisher` resolves to the catalog's ID. 0.2 `started.result.consent` is read as `consented`.
- CATALOG.md §2.3: a host interprets learner events in the catalog they name, so the order of connected catalogs never changes a record's meaning.
- SPEC.md §6.1, §7: objectives are `{ id, text }`; question blocks name the `objectives` they check. Schemas: element and path objectives accept both forms, question kinds take `objectives`, events take the new verbs and fields, mastery takes `completed[]` and `target_level`.
- MCP server: everything is derived from `events.jsonl`; `record_progress` checks `completed` (requires `level`, picks evidence from the log when not given, explains what is missing), fills `result.objectives` from the question block and `context.version`/`catalog` from the library, accepts `skipped`, `consented`, `withdrew`, `level_changed`; the `mastery` override argument is removed; new `sync_learner` tool.

- All documents brought down to their essentials (about a quarter shorter overall; PEDAGOGY.md and the skills about half), with contradictions resolved and more left to the tutor's judgment. SPEC.md describes the format only; PEDAGOGY.md 0.2 describes the learning atmosphere, seven rules that always hold and defaults for courses, steps, media, review and writing content (simulator design rules now live only in SIMULATOR-SPEC.md §7); the tutor and author skills are about half as long. Section numbers that tools refer to are unchanged, except PEDAGOGY.md, which is renumbered.
- SPEC.md §6.7: one order of precedence: content policy and safety, binding content, the learner's explicit choices, tutor notes, profile, PEDAGOGY.md defaults.
- SPEC.md §5.1: an element lists at least one level (was two); two adjacent ones are recommended, and a tutor may teach other levels from Core, Key facts and Misconceptions. Schema `levels.minItems` is 1.
- SPEC.md §10, LEARNER-RECORD.md §4, §6: only evidence (`answered`, `assessed`, `completed`, `station_completed`) changes mastery; self-reports such as `understood` are kept but never count, also not as a measure of how well a level text works. One shared threshold: mastery 0.8 or more is "secure" and needs evidence for every objective; the fixed 0.4 cap and the 3-day first review are now "below secure" and "an early first review". The MCP server no longer counts `understood` as 0.8.
- LEARNER-RECORD.md: storage is up to the host and a database is recommended; operations, events and the export folder are normative.
- CODE-SPEC.md §5: a host that cannot reach the learner's machine shows a lab's files and commands instead of copying them; the tutor skill uses `copy_code_lab` only where the host offers it.
- Tutor skill: no profile questionnaire at the start; preferences are learned as the learner states them.

- LEARNER-RECORD.md 0.2: the record is defined by operations, not files. `get_learner` returns a small overview (profile, courses, up to 10 reviews due, ordered by what the next step needs); `get_learner({ course })` returns one path or element in detail. Hosts store the record as they like (Magister: PostgreSQL); the folder `profile.md`, `mastery.json`, `events.jsonl` is the export format. New: courses derived from events (§5), sharing per course instead of per platform (§7), sync between copies by event `id` (§8), recap after a break as `assessed` with `context.reason: recap` (§6). `consents.json` is gone.
- Tutor skill: overview first, course details when working on a course, recap after two weeks, sharing shown before a course starts, `note` and `keep`.
- MCP server: `get_learner` takes `course` and no longer returns all mastery and the whole progress; `record_progress` takes `path`, `note`, `keep`, `context_reason` and the verbs `started` and `left`.

### Added
- `conformance/learner-record/two-devices.json`: two copies changed independently and the state every host must derive after merging them (LEARNER-RECORD.md §9), run by `mcp/test/learner-conformance.test.mjs`, also through two local copies syncing.
- `ptf-check`: errors for unknown or duplicate objective IDs in questions and elements; warnings for objectives without ID, check questions without `objectives`, and objectives without a check question per level.
- Minimal example: `demo/counting` 0.4.0 with objective IDs, questions that name them and a highschool `free_text` check; path shares with `publisher`; bundle 0.2.0.
- CATALOG.md (draft 0.1): catalogs, so that a host such as Magister can connect several libraries, public or behind an access key, and store each package version once. A library publishes `ptf-catalog.json` with its packages (bundles, simulators, code labs), each with a tree `digest`, delivered as one snapshot archive or per-package archives; `auth` is `none`, `bearer` or (reserved) `oauth2`; the listing per learner is the access; hosts recheck it every `recheck_hours` and on login and hide everything on 401/403; `cache` (`shared`, `per-user`, `none`) says what hosts may store; catalogs never receive learner data. New `schemas/catalog.schema.json`; `ptf-check catalog [--write] <library>` regenerates the package list and `ptf-check` fails when `ptf-catalog.json` is stale. The minimal example has a catalog. SPEC.md §3 points to it.
- SPEC.md §6.1, §8.1: prerequisites and steps from another library as `{ id, catalog }` / `{ element, catalog }`, so a host knows where to find them and the tutor can offer to connect the library. `ptf-check` does not resolve them locally.
- SPEC.md §8.2 `sharing` on paths (`with`, `areas`, `mode: required | optional`); §8.3 what a path requires and reaches, derived from its steps; §8.4 when a path is completed. Schemas: path `sharing` and step `catalog`, element `requires` objects, event verbs `started` and `left`, `result.consent`, `note`, mastery `keep` and `completed`, profile goals `paths`. Minimal example path declares optional sharing.
- PEDAGOGY.md §8.3: recap after a break.
- Prior knowledge before a course (SPEC.md §8.1): the tutor lets the learner tell what they know, probes where knowledge shows up and proposes which steps to skip and at which level to start; a step is skipped only when every objective is backed by a correct answer. New question `purpose: probe` (counts as a `check` for `ptf-check`), optional path heading `# Prior knowledge`, learner event verb `assessed` that sets mastery to the placement score with `source: "diagnosis"` and a first review after at most 3 days (LEARNER-RECORD.md §4, §5; schemas). The `ptf-tutor` skill gets a new section 1b for the conversation, `record_progress` accepts `assessed`, and the `ptf-author` skill mentions probe questions. Minimal example: `demo/counting` 0.3.0 has a `probe` question and the path a `# Prior knowledge` section.
- Learning goals in the content (SPEC.md §6.1, §6.7, §8): `objectives` for learning elements and paths (two to four checkable statements), optional `# Introduction` and `# Tutor notes` headings, and `use_when` on simulator entries. New §6.7 says what is binding (objectives, Core, Key facts, Misconceptions, tutor notes) and what is only a recommendation (level texts, examples, questions, simulators); authors who want a fixed sequence or fixed texts say so in `# Tutor notes`. `ptf-check` warns about elements without `objectives`.
- Explanation preferences in the learner profile (LEARNER-RECORD.md §3.1): `style.explain` with `start_with`, `analogies`, `formulas`, `length`, `simulators` and `notes`.
- PEDAGOGY.md: "Say where you are going" and "Guide the discovery" principles, an Orient phase at the start of each step and course, and simulators as aids rather than mandatory stations.
- MCP server: `get_learner` returns `progress` (completed elements and completed simulator stations, from the whole event log); `show_simulator` tells the tutor which stations the learner already completed; `list_library` and `search_library` include `objectives` and `use_when`; profile updates merge nested objects, so a partial `style.explain` update keeps the other fields.
- SIMULATOR-SPEC.md §7 rule 5 "Predict, observe, explain": a station that runs an experiment keeps its controls locked until the learner picks a prediction, gives no hint while picking, judges the prediction only after the result and reports it as `answered`.
- SIMULATOR-SPEC.md §4.7: simulators start focused on their first station, also standalone, and show progress with direct go-to per station.
- SIMULATOR-SPEC.md §4.5: canvas and WebGL simulators resolve theme tokens through the browser, because host values such as `light-dark()` turn canvas drawings black.
- `ptf-selftest` checks the tutor contract: new steps `expectStations` (exact visible stations, for focus mode), `snapshot: {contains}` (the snapshot reports what the learner did), `reload` (resume round trip with the last `ptf/state`) and `expectEvent: {verb, station}`. For every package it also fails when `sim.json` declares no events or a focused `goto` shows more than one station, and warns when a declared event is never triggered; superseded versions only get notes (SIMULATOR-SPEC.md §6).
- SPEC.md §6.6 subject anchors: learning elements name the concepts they teach as Wikidata items in `about` (first entry = primary concept) instead of a PTF taxonomy. Schema field `about`; `ptf-check` warns when a `reviewed` or `proven` element has no `about` and when two elements of one bundle share a primary concept. New `tools/ptf-wikidata.mjs` (`search` for candidates, `verify` a library's IDs and labels against Wikidata). The MCP server lists `about` and searches its labels.
- PEDAGOGY.md (recommendation, not conformance): evidence-based principles for course design (anchor in prior knowledge, try before tell, one idea per step, simple-then-honest explanations, concrete to abstract, dual coding, misconception confrontation, retrieval, spacing and interleaving, self-explanation, mastery), the arc of one step mapped to PTF fields, granularity rules for elements, paths and simulators, and the research behind each rule. Notes that matching "learning styles" is not supported by evidence. Referenced from SIMULATOR-SPEC.md §7 and the `ptf-author` and `ptf-tutor` skills.
- CONTENT-POLICY.md §1.2 "Contributor responsibility and license": contributors are responsible for their content and its legal compliance, license each contribution under the library's licenses by submitting it, and are the human behind any agent; maintainers remove infringing content on notice. `ptf-author` adds the contributor statement to every PR.
- CONTENT-POLICY.md §1.1 "Sources and original work": any material may be a source; content is written fresh (no copied or closely paraphrased text, no translated text, no reproduced exercises or figures); CC0/BY/BY-SA material may be adapted with attribution, NC/ND material only in private libraries; agents must not rewrite sources and must respect terms that forbid AI processing.
- CODE-SPEC.md: code labs, versioned packages of runnable code in steps (`code/<name>/<version>/` with `lab.json`, README and one complete file per step) that learners copy into their own workspace, run and extend. Elements refer to them with `code: [{ ref, step }]`; `ptf.lock` pins them under `code`. Safety rules for hosts (never run automatically, copy before editing, show dependencies first) and for labs (no network, processes, string eval or unsafe deserialization).
- `schemas/code-manifest.schema.json`; `learning-element` and `lockfile` schemas accept `code`.
- `ptf-check` validates code labs (manifest, step files, data files, integrity, forbidden APIs) and code refs; `hash` and `lock` handle labs. New `tools/ptf-codetest.mjs` runs every step in smoke mode in a temporary copy.
- Example library: `code/counting/1.0.0`, referenced from `counting.md`.
- MCP server: `get_code_lab` and `copy_code_lab` (verified copy into `~/.ptf/workspace/`, never overwrites, never runs code; `PTF_WORKSPACE`); `list_library` and `search_library` include code labs; tutor skill offers a lab step after an element, author skill covers labs.
- `mcp/`: `show_question` tool and quiz card (MCP App) for `multiple_choice` and `free_text` question blocks, from a learning element or written by the model and validated against the question-block schema; the card checks multiple-choice answers, records them and sends them to the chat; free-text answers go to the chat for grading.
- Minimal example: `multiple_choice` question in `demo/counting`.
- SIMULATOR-SPEC.md §8 (proposal) libraries and vendoring: when to use no library, an own integrator, `planck` or `three-core`; the allowlist (`vendor/allowlist.json`) with reproducible build recipes; rules for vendoring byte-identical classic-script builds into `vendor/`; WebGL fallbacks; physics correctness rules (SI units, fixed time step, closed-form checks in `test.json`, honest exaggeration). §7 build rules 1, 11 and new 12 refer to it.
- `tools/ptf-vendor.mjs`: `list`, `add <sim-dir> <build>@<version>` (copies the build and declares it in `sim.json`), `build <build>@<version> [--write]` (reproduces a build from its recipe).
- `vendor/`: allowlisted builds `three-core@0.170.0` (tree-shaken three.js, global `THREE`) and `planck@1.4.2` (global `planck`) with recipes and licenses.
- `sim.json` field `vendor` (schema). `ptf-check` verifies vendored builds against the manifest and the allowlist, rejects undeclared scripts in `vendor/`, skips the forbidden-pattern scan for verified builds, leaves them out of the 300 KB recommendation, and warns about `<script type="module">`.
- `mcp/`: local MCP server (stdio, `dist/ptf-mcp.mjs` single file) that clones and serves a PTF library, shows simulators inline as MCP Apps (player page embeds the unmodified package via `ptf-host.js` in a `sandbox="allow-scripts"` srcdoc iframe, after an integrity check), and keeps the learner record in `~/.ptf/learner/` (events, mastery with spaced-review dates, profile, simulator resume state).
- `ptf-tutor` skill and MCP prompt (teaching method, learning-goal check) and `ptf-author` skill (change or add content, run checks, open a pull request); Claude Code plugin and marketplace entry.
- CONTENT-POLICY.md: what shared libraries accept, automated review gates and quality rubric for contributions without manual review.
- PTF v0.1 draft: SPEC.md (bundles, learning elements, four default levels plus custom levels, question blocks, learning paths, IDs, versioning, conformance).
- SIMULATOR-SPEC.md: `sim.json` manifest, integrity hash, semver rules, JSON-RPC-over-postMessage host contract, security requirements, self-test format, build rules.
- LEARNER-RECORD.md: local-first learner profile, events, mastery and consents.
- JSON Schemas for bundle index, learning element, learning path, question block, simulator manifest, lockfile, learner profile, learner event, mastery.
- `runtime/ptf-sim.js` (simulator side) and `runtime/ptf-host.js` (host side).
- `tools/ptf-check.mjs` validator with `hash` and `lock` commands; `tools/ptf-selftest.mjs` sandboxed browser self-test and dev host.
- Minimal example library and tests.
- SPEC.md §6.5 `# Further reading`: optional deep-dive links (e.g. the source video with timestamps) offered after the lesson; `ptf-check` no longer warns about source references in that section.
- SIMULATOR-SPEC.md §4.7 focus mode: one idea per station, `focus` in the init context and in `ptf/goto`, Back/Next with `experienced` events; `ptf-sim.js` passes `{ focus }` to `onGoto`, `ptf-host.js` `goto(station, { focus })`, `ptf-selftest` accepts `goto: {station, focus}`; MCP `show_simulator` takes `focus` (default on when a station is given) and the tutor skill walks station by station and offers `# Further reading` after a step.
- SIMULATOR-SPEC.md §4.6 UI languages: how a simulator picks its UI language from `locale`, fallback to the first `lang` entry, English snapshots and events, and a markup pattern for bilingual simulators.

### Changed
- PEDAGOGY.md reworked (still a recommendation): a reader guide (§0); sixteen principles in four groups, with new principles on finding out what the learner knows, worked examples and expertise reversal, explanatory feedback with hints, and judging understanding by evidence instead of self-report (constructive alignment); a course introduction that answers six questions and a session arc (§2); "Choosing the medium" with when a simulator earns its place and when to skip it (§4); every objective tested by a `check` question at every level (§5); simulator stations that fit one phone screen and are framed even without a tutor (§7); progress, feedback and review behaviour (§8); checklists for elements, paths, simulators and tutor sessions (§9); new sources; a prior-knowledge conversation before a new course (§2.2): the learner tells what they know, the tutor probes where knowledge shows up and proposes which steps to skip and at which level to start, skipping only with evidence. SIMULATOR-SPEC.md §7 points to the new section numbers.
- Levels are depths, not parts (SPEC.md §5.2, new): every level of an element covers Core, every objective and the central misconceptions, so a learner who works through one level has the whole concept; higher levels add terms, formulas, limits and harder tasks but no missing piece. Level texts must stand on their own (§6.3). Simulator stations that teach part of a concept are available at every level and change depth inside; restricted station `levels` are only for stations of elements without the lower levels or for optional deepening (SIMULATOR-SPEC.md §2, §7 rule 4); offering a station at more levels is a minor version. Custom levels move to §5.3. PEDAGOGY.md, the `ptf-author` and `ptf-tutor` skills and the `get_element` description say the same ("go deeper" = the same concept one level up, never the next part).
- `ptf-check` warns when a level of an element has no `check` question, and when an element has level texts for some of its levels but not for all.
- Minimal example: `demo/counting` 0.2.0 gets a highschool level text and a highschool `check` question.
- `ptf-tutor` skill: a session introduces the course (goal, what the learner will be able to do, where they are) before the first step; each step starts with its objective, opens a simulator only when it helps (`use_when`, `style.explain.simulators`), says what to look for and always debriefs what the learner saw; completed steps and stations are recapped instead of restarted; explanation preferences are asked once and updated whenever the learner states them. `ptf-author` asks for objectives, an introduction and `use_when`.

### Fixed
- MCP player: host colour tokens are resolved to plain `rgb()` before they reach a simulator.
